Privacy

Uravu (உறவு): what is stored, where, and who can see it. Last updated 21 November 2026.

Where your data is kept

You sign in to use Uravu. Everything you add (people, relationships, dates, likes, notes, functions, guest lists, moi and documents) is saved in your account, in a database run on Supabase, so it’s the same on every device you sign in on.

Nothing about your family is stored on your phone or computer. While Uravu is open, your data is only in the app’s memory, and it’s gone when you close it. The device remembers just two things: that you’re signed in, and (once you’ve entered it) the key that opens your document vault. Without internet you can look at what’s already open, but changes can’t be saved.

  • Your account: your email address, and your password stored by Supabase in hashed form. If you use Google, Google tells us your email address.
  • Your data: people, relationships, dates, likes, notes, functions, guests and moi, stored as you entered them.
  • Who can read it: only you, when signed in. Every table has row-level security, so the database refuses to show one person’s rows to anyone else. The people who run the server (the app’s owner and Supabase) could technically read these rows with administrator access. That is why documents are locked separately (below).

The document vault

Document numbers (Aadhaar, PAN, policy numbers, licence and others), document notes and names, and every document photo and PDF are encrypted on your device before they are uploaded. The server only ever receives the locked version and cannot open it. Nobody else can either, including the app’s owner, Supabase, or anyone who gets a copy of the database.

  • The lock is AES-256-GCM. The key is made on your device from your vault PIN (PBKDF2 with SHA-256, 600,000 rounds). The PIN is never sent anywhere, and the key is never sent unlocked. If you change your PIN, the same key is locked with your new PIN and that locked copy is saved in your account.
  • Files go into a private storage area with one folder per person. They are fetched only through links that expire after one minute.
  • If you forget your vault PIN, the vault can’t be opened or recovered, by you or anyone else. There is no other copy of the locked documents, so write the PIN down somewhere safe.
  • A short PIN is easier to guess for someone who has a copy of the locked data. A longer PIN or passphrase is much safer.

Not locked: the document type (for example “Vehicle insurance”), whose document it is, and the reminder setting, so the app can show renewals. Policy dates and amounts are inside the locked part.

What is never collected

  • No advertising, no tracking, no analytics, and nothing is sold or shared.
  • The only other outside service is Google Fonts, which loads the app’s fonts.
  • “Ask Uravu” (AI) is switched off in this version.

Your choices

  • Download all your data: Settings › Account › Download all my data (or Settings › Save a backup file). The file includes document photos. Keep it somewhere safe: it is not encrypted.
  • Sign out: the app forgets your sign-in and the vault key on that device. Nothing else was stored there.
  • Delete your account: Settings › Account › Delete my account and all data. This removes your account, every synced row and every vault file from the server straight away. Supabase’s own backups of the database expire on their normal schedule.
